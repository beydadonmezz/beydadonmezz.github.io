LINGRID

Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 27, 2026

This policy explains how the LINGRID mobile app ("the app") and its developer, Beydanur Dönmez ("we", "us"), handle information. The app has no account system. Our only server of our own is for the shared leaderboard (section 4); in addition, RevenueCat processes a record of each purchase for us on its own servers (section 8). It uses advertising and consent services from Google, and advertising from Unity Ads and Liftoff Monetize through Google's AdMob mediation. To measure which of our own ads bring new players, it also uses, only with your consent, the Meta SDK (Meta Platforms), Google Analytics for Firebase (Google) and the AppsFlyer SDK (AppsFlyer) (section 7). Also only with your consent, it uses PostHog to see where players get stuck (section 7, "Game statistics"). The shared leaderboard, game statistics and ad measurement depend on your answer to one question in the app, "Sıralamaya katıl" (Join the leaderboard) (section 4). On iPhone and iPad it uses Apple's purchase (App Store), iCloud sync and Game Center services; on Android it uses Google Play's purchase (Google Play Billing), Google Play Games and Android backup services, each under the terms described below.

1. Who is responsible

Beydanur Dönmez, the developer and publisher of the app, is responsible for the app and for this policy. You can reach us at lingrid.sandbox@outlook.com.

2. Data stored only on your device

To make the game work, the app stores the following on your device, in the app's own storage: your puzzle progress and best scores, daily puzzle results and streak, your diamond balance and its transaction history, whether you have purchased Remove Ads or Lifetime Unlimited, which colour themes you have unlocked, the free-puzzle allowance for the day, puzzles you have started, your sound, music, colour theme and appearance settings, and your answer on the "Sıralamaya katıl" screen (section 4) with when you gave it, where (the window, the Leaderboard tab or Settings) and which version of its text you were shown (text version number, region and platform variant). This data does not leave your device through the app, except as described in "Sync and backup" below, and we cannot see it. It is deleted when you delete the app, unless a copy has been kept in your iCloud account through iCloud sync on iPhone and iPad, or in your Google account's backup through Android backup on Android. Your device's own backups (for example iCloud Backup) may also include it under Apple's control. There are three exceptions, kept so that deleting and reinstalling the app on the same device does not lose them: (1) the number of free puzzles and rewarded-ad allowances you have used that day (three numbers and that day's date), so that the daily allowances do not start over; (2) the daily puzzles you have solved with their scores, your streak days and your total score; and (3) your shared-leaderboard device credentials: the random identifier and key the app created for this device, and your leaderboard choice (joined, not joined, or your record is hidden; section 4). These records are kept in the device keychain on iOS and in Google Play services' Block Store on Android. They do not contain your name, email address or contact details. Records (1) and (2) are used only on your device (scores of solved puzzles reach the shared leaderboard only as described in section 4); record (3) is used only to connect to the shared leaderboard as described in section 4. On iOS they stay on that device and are not synced to iCloud. On Android the app does not add them to your Google cloud backup, but Google Play services may copy them to a new device when you move your data directly from the old one. Record (1) is replaced the next time you open the app on a later day.

3. Game Center (Apple)

If you are signed in to Apple Game Center, the app sends the following to Apple so that leaderboards and achievements work: your daily puzzle score together with that day's puzzle number, your all-time total score, and your achievement progress. Your Game Center nickname and scores can be visible to other players on leaderboards. Game Center is operated by Apple under Apple's Privacy Policy; you can manage or turn it off in your device's Settings. The app uses your Game Center identity only to show whether you are signed in; it does not store it or send it anywhere else.

Google Play Games (Android). On Android, leaderboards and achievements run on Google Play Games services. If you are signed in to Google Play Games on your device, the app sends Google your daily puzzle score (as a number that includes that day's date) with the puzzle number, your all-time total score and the achievements you earn. Your Play Games player name and scores may be visible to other players on the leaderboards. Google processes this data under the Google Privacy Policy. If you do not sign in, the game works the same; only leaderboards and achievements are unavailable.

4. Shared leaderboard

The Leaderboard (Sıralama) tab has a shared leaderboard where iPhone and Android players compete together. From version 1.4.0 up to the 1.4.x versions every player was on it automatically. From version 1.5.0 you are on it only if you choose to join (see "One choice: Sıralamaya katıl" below). If you do not join, the game works exactly the same.

One choice: "Sıralamaya katıl" (Join the leaderboard). From version 1.5.0 the app asks one question, "Sıralamaya katıl", in a small window that opens from the bottom of the screen. The app always opens normally; the window is not shown at the first launch. It appears when you return to the home screen after finishing your first puzzle; if you open the Leaderboard (Sıralama) tab before that, it appears there; if neither happens, it appears at the second launch. The window is short: one sentence, "Sıralamada adınla yer al; oyunu geliştirmek ve reklamlarımızı ölçmek için nasıl oynadığını görmemize izin ver." (Be on the leaderboard with your name; let us see how you play, to improve the game and to measure our ads.); a short note that names where the data goes (our leaderboard server in Germany, and PostHog, Meta, Google and AppsFlyer), says that they are all outside Türkiye and that your data may not be protected there as well as in Türkiye, and that you can turn this off in Settings at any time; an "Ayrıntılar" (Details) link; and two buttons of the same size and look, "Katıl" (Join) and "Katılma" (Don't join). "Ayrıntılar" opens a details page in the app, "Sıralama ve ölçüm" (Leaderboard and measurement). For each of the three processing operations that "Katıl" turns on together it says what is sent, to which company and why: (a) the shared leaderboard (this section); (b) game statistics with PostHog (section 7, "Game statistics"); (c) the measurement of our own ads with Meta, Google and AppsFlyer (section 7). It also covers the transfer abroad, purchase records (RevenueCat, section 8), the fact that points earned while you are not on the leaderboard are not added to it, Apple's tracking permission on iPhone and iPad, and the retention periods, and it links to this policy. These three are separate processing operations; the data, recipients, places and periods of each are described separately in this policy, which is the full and binding description. If you choose "Katıl", all three start together. If you choose "Katılma", none of the three starts, nothing described for them is sent, and the rest of the game works fully: puzzles, diamonds, purchases, Game Center and Play Games. Closing the window without choosing (swiping it down or tapping outside it) is not an answer: it counts neither as "Katıl" nor as "Katılma". The app then shows the window once more at the next suitable moment, at most twice in total; after that you can still join at any time in the Leaderboard tab or in Settings. Until you choose, none of the three runs: nothing is sent to the leaderboard server, PostHog, Meta, Google or AppsFlyer. The scores of puzzles you solve before choosing stay on your device: they are sent to the leaderboard if you choose "Katıl", and never if you choose "Katılma". If you used a version before 1.5.0, the same rules apply after the update; until you answer, nothing new is sent for any of the three.

Changing your mind: one switch in Settings (Ayarlar), "Sıralama ve ölçüm" (Leaderboard and measurement), turns all three off or on together. Turning it off withdraws your consent, at any time and as easily as you gave it: from then on nothing is sent for any of the three (what happens to your leaderboard record is described in "Leaving and deleting" below; data sent earlier to PostHog, Meta, Google and AppsFlyer is kept as described in section 7). Turning it on again has the same effect as "Katıl". The information link next to the switch opens the same details page as "Ayrıntılar".

iPhone and iPad: right after "Katıl", Apple's App Tracking Transparency (ATT) prompt (section 6) asks you separately whether the app may track you. Your answer there does not affect the leaderboard or game statistics: if you do not allow tracking, you stay on the leaderboard and game statistics continue. Our ad measurement then does not use Meta or Google: the Meta SDK and Google Analytics for Firebase are not started and nothing is sent to Meta or Google for it. Only AppsFlyer measures our ads, without your advertising identifier (IDFA), with Apple's SKAdNetwork and AdServices information and the vendor identifier (IDFV), as described in section 7.

EEA, UK and Switzerland: there, "Katıl" turns on the shared leaderboard and game statistics. The window's sentence there is "Sıralamada adınla yer al; oyunu geliştirmek için nasıl oynadığını görmemize izin ver." (Be on the leaderboard with your name; let us see how you play, to improve the game.), its note names only our leaderboard server in Germany and PostHog, and the details page does not describe ad measurement. Whether Google Analytics for Firebase may measure our ads there is decided, as before, only in Google's consent message (UMP, section 5); the Meta SDK and the AppsFlyer SDK are never used there (section 7).

What is sent while you are on the leaderboard: the score, time and number of hints of the puzzles you solve; a random identifier the app creates for this device (the app keeps it, with a device key, in the iOS keychain or, on Android, in Google Play services' Block Store, so that after a reinstall on the same device you keep the same leaderboard record. On Android this needs the device's backup to be on; if you are signed in to Game Center or Play Games, your record also comes back through that sign-in; section 2); and, if you are signed in to Game Center or Play Games, your display name there (if you are not signed in, you get an automatic name). Your Game Center or Play Games identity is stored only as a one-way hash. At the first launch of version 1.4.0, the scores of puzzles solved earlier on the device were sent once, so that the total was complete. The scores of puzzles you solve while you are not on the leaderboard (after "Katılma", with "Sıralama ve ölçüm" off, or while your record is hidden) are never sent, and they are not added later when you join again. Your email address, location and contacts are not sent. Your name and scores are visible to other players on the leaderboard. From version 1.4.2, to check that requests come from the unmodified LINGRID app on a genuine device, the app also sends an integrity proof about once a day when the device supports it: on iPhone and iPad an Apple App Attest proof (a key Apple creates on the device and Apple's certificate for it, or a signature made with that key), on Android a Google Play Integrity token. The server keeps the App Attest key's public key and identifier with your device record; it does not store the proofs or tokens themselves. In this version the result of the check is only recorded; it does not change your scores or your place on the leaderboard.

Why: to show the leaderboard, to remove cheating scores (the server recalculates every score with the game's own formula) and to protect the leaderboard from abuse (hourly request counts and integrity checks).

Where: on a server that we rent from Contabo GmbH, in a data centre in Germany (European Union), that is, outside Türkiye. The connection between the app and the server is encrypted (HTTPS). Only authorised administrators can access the server. Versions of the app before 1.5.0 reach this server through the servers of Supabase, Inc. in Frankfurt (Germany, European Union), which only pass the requests on. A copy of the leaderboard data as it was on November 27, 2026 stays on Supabase; it is not otherwise updated, but deletions are applied to it too, and it is deleted as a whole at the latest on 31 March 2027. Every day the server makes a backup of the leaderboard data; the backup is encrypted before it leaves the server, only we can open it, and it is stored in Google's Google Drive service. When the app connects, your IP address may appear in short-lived technical logs kept for at most 14 days. To confirm a Game Center or Play Games sign-in, the server checks it with Apple or Google. To create and check integrity proofs, Apple (App Attest) and Google (Play Integrity) process technical data about your device and the app.

How long: daily puzzle scores for 35 days. Your total score and name, unless you delete them earlier, are deleted at the latest 12 months after your last connection, that is, after the app last contacted our server. This also applies to hidden records: for a hidden record the 12 months start at the moment it was hidden, because the app does not contact the server while your record is hidden. To prevent abuse, the server keeps the hourly number of requests linked to your player identifier for 14 days; these records are deleted when that period ends or when you delete your leaderboard data. The result of the integrity check (passed, failed, not sent) is kept for 14 days, linked to your player identifier; it is deleted when you delete your leaderboard data. Encrypted backups are kept for 30 days and then deleted. The copy on Supabase (see "Where") is not otherwise updated, but the 35-day and 12-month deletions and your own deletion requests are applied to it too; it is deleted as a whole when the move is complete, at the latest on 31 March 2027. A short record of each deletion (player identifier, kind of deletion, time, number of records deleted) is kept for at most 180 days (device link records likewise), to prove that the deletions were done and to carry them over if we ever move back to Supabase.

Leaving and deleting: these are two different things. Not joining or turning off ("Katılma" or the "Sıralama ve ölçüm" switch): if you have a leaderboard record, it is not deleted but hidden: from that moment you and your scores no longer appear on the leaderboard, in its player counts or in other players' ranks, and nothing new is sent. If you choose "Katıl" again (or turn the switch on), your record comes back exactly as it was, with your old total score and place; puzzles solved in between are not added. A hidden record follows the same rules on the server as any other record ("How long" above): its daily puzzle rows are deleted after 35 days (their points stay in the total), and if you do not join again, the whole record is deleted automatically 12 months after it was hidden. If you never joined, you have no leaderboard record and nothing is sent to the leaderboard server. Deleting ("Sıralama verilerimi sil", Delete my leaderboard data): this option in Settings (Ayarlar) deletes everything about you on the leaderboard server at once, whether your record is visible or hidden, and takes you off the leaderboard; the copies in the encrypted backups are deleted automatically within 30 days. We also delete your data from the old copy on Supabase. After that, unless you join again, the app sends nothing to the leaderboard server (if you are offline, only the deletion request is sent once a connection is back); if you join again, a new record starts. You can also write to us.

5. Advertising (Google Mobile Ads)

The app may show a short, full-screen interstitial ad between puzzles — when you leave the results screen of a puzzle you have just finished — but never while you are actively solving one. It also offers optional "rewarded" ads, shown only when you choose to watch them: on the home screen and in the diamond shop in exchange for diamonds, and on a locked level puzzle to open that puzzle. If you buy Remove Ads, the between-puzzle interstitial ads stop; the rewarded ads stay available because you choose to watch them. These ads are requested through Google's Mobile Ads SDK (Google AdMob). Through AdMob mediation, two other advertising companies can also bid to show an ad, using their own SDKs, which are part of the app: Unity Ads (Unity Technologies; privacy policy) and Liftoff Monetize (Liftoff Mobile, Inc., formerly Vungle; privacy policy). To request, show and measure ads, Google, these companies and their advertising partners may process: an approximate, coarse location estimated from your IP address; non-user performance data such as app launch time, crash reports and hang rate, which is used for diagnostics and may also be used to help decide which ads to show, including by sharing it with other companies that display ads; a device identifier — your device's advertising identifier where allowed, or another app- or developer-bound identifier — used for third-party advertising, our own advertising and analytics; and information about which ads you have seen and how you use the app, such as app launches. Unity's own disclosure also lists a user ID assigned by its SDK, purchase history, other usage data and device details such as language, model, screen and connection type. These lists are the companies' own disclosures of what their SDKs can collect, not our choice; they receive the data directly from their SDKs, and it does not pass through us. See "How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services" and Google's Privacy Policy. Before showing ads to a user in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, the app shows Google's consent message (the "UMP" form) so you can choose whether to allow personalised advertising; your choice is passed on to Unity Ads and Liftoff Monetize as well.

6. App Tracking Transparency and non-personalised ads

On iPhone and iPad, the app asks for your permission under Apple's App Tracking Transparency (ATT) framework, shown in Turkish and in English, only right after you choose "Katıl" (on the "Sıralamaya katıl" screen, in the Leaderboard tab, or by turning on "Sıralama ve ölçüm" in Settings; section 4), and only if it has not been asked before; this is the same in every region, including the EEA, the UK and Switzerland. If you choose "Katılma" or have not answered the "Sıralamaya katıl" screen yet, Apple's prompt is not shown and, as long as you have not allowed tracking, only non-personalised ads are requested. Your ATT choice also decides whether the measurement of our own ads (section 7) may use your advertising identifier; it does not decide whether you are on the leaderboard. Outside the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, if you decline tracking, the Meta SDK and Google Analytics for Firebase are not started at all and only AppsFlyer measures our ads, without the advertising identifier (section 7). Non-personalised ads still use the advertising SDK data described above (for example, coarse location and a non-tracking device identifier), because the advertising SDKs need that data to request, serve, measure and protect any ad, personalised or not. You can change your ATT choice at any time in your device's Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking; where the EEA/UK/Switzerland consent message applies, you can change your choice from the link Google provides inside that message.

Android has no App Tracking Transparency. On Android you can reset or delete your advertising ID in Settings > Privacy > Ads (or Google > Ads); if the advertising ID is deleted or UMP consent is not given, only non-personalised ads are requested. The measurement SDKs in section 7 never collect your Android advertising ID, anywhere.

AppsFlyer combines the information from Meta, Google and Apple Ads into one count of installations and purchases per ad; it processes this data on our behalf, under a data processing agreement and only on our instructions. For the records AppsFlyer keeps itself while running its service (log data), AppsFlyer's own Services Privacy Policy applies. They are not used to choose the ads shown inside the app, and we do not build advertising audiences from this data. "Game statistics" at the end of this section is a separate purpose and is described separately.

Only with your consent: outside the EEA, the UK and Switzerland (on Android and on iPhone and iPad), the SDKs in this section are started only after you choose "Katıl" (Join) on the "Sıralamaya katıl" screen (section 4); the window names Meta, Google and AppsFlyer, and its details page describes this measurement. On iPhone and iPad, Apple's ATT prompt (section 6) follows "Katıl" as a separate question; if you do not allow tracking there, the Meta SDK and Google Analytics for Firebase are not started either, and only AppsFlyer runs ("When your advertising identifier is used" below). Data sent before you withdrew your consent is kept by Meta, Google and AppsFlyer as described below. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent message is used for ad measurement instead, and neither the Meta SDK nor the AppsFlyer SDK is used there (see below).

What is sent: that the app was installed and opened for the first time; app opens and sessions; purchases (the product, its price and currency, and the transaction number, so that one purchase is counted only once); and that you completed a puzzle (only whether it was the daily puzzle or a level puzzle; no answers, times or scores). Your email address, phone number and any other data you enter are never used for this, and it is not active in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland. Your name, email address, contacts, precise location, leaderboard name and your Game Center or Play Games identity are not sent. This data does not name you, but it is not anonymous: it is pseudonymous data tied to this installation of the app.

AppsFlyer: with your consent, the AppsFlyer SDK sends AppsFlyer the same kinds of events (installation and first opening, app opens, purchases with the product, its price and currency and the transaction number, and puzzle completions as described above), together with an identifier AppsFlyer creates for this installation, the app version, device and system information (such as model, operating system version, language and mobile network operator) and your IP address. On Android, if the Facebook, Facebook Lite or Instagram app is installed on your phone, it also asks those apps for Meta's installation source information (Meta Install Referrer) to tell whether the installation came from a Meta ad. On iPhone and iPad it collects your advertising identifier (IDFA) only if you have also allowed tracking in Apple's ATT prompt. When your advertising identifier is used: on iPhone and iPad, only if you have chosen "Katıl" (section 4) and also allowed tracking in Apple's ATT prompt (section 6). EEA, UK and Switzerland: there, "Katıl" covers only the shared leaderboard and game statistics (section 4); the app never starts the Meta SDK or the AppsFlyer SDK there, and nothing is sent to Meta or AppsFlyer from there. Where: on Meta's and Google's servers, mainly in the United States and in Ireland; on AppsFlyer's servers in the European Union and the United States (Amazon Web Services and Google Cloud), and AppsFlyer's group companies in Israel, the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany, India, Japan, China and Hong Kong may access the data to run and support the service; that is, outside Türkiye. Your choices: everywhere, the "Sıralama ve ölçüm" (Leaderboard and measurement) switch in Settings (Ayarlar): turning it off stops all measurement from then on, together with the leaderboard and game statistics (section 4); on iPhone and iPad, Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking for your advertising identifier; in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, the consent message; reinstalling the app gives the SDKs new installation identifiers. Measurement runs only in the versions of the app published on the App Store and Google Play.

Game statistics (PostHog). To find out which parts of LINGRID are hard or confusing and where players stop, the app can send usage events to PostHog (PostHog, Inc., United States; it stores the data on its EU servers in Frankfurt, Germany). PostHog processes the data only on our instructions, under a data processing agreement. This data is not used for advertising and is not shared with advertising companies.

Only with your consent: game statistics are one of the three processing operations that "Katıl" (Join) on the "Sıralamaya katıl" screen turns on together (section 4); the window names PostHog, and its details page describes game statistics. This data does not name you, but it is not anonymous: it is pseudonymous data tied to this installation of the app.

Why: to improve the game: to find the levels that feel hard and the places where players get stuck or stop. How long: at most 1 year, then deleted. If you want data sent earlier to be deleted sooner, copy your identifier with Settings (Ayarlar) → "Kimliğimi kopyala" (Copy my identifier) and send it to us; we delete the data linked to it. Where: stored in Germany (European Union), that is, outside Türkiye; PostHog, Inc. and its sub-processors may access it from outside Türkiye, including the United States. This transfer rests on the explicit consent you give with "Katıl" (section 14).

8. In-app purchases

The app offers in-app purchases — two one-time purchases, Remove Ads and Lifetime Unlimited (which may be offered at a lower price in the same store to those who own Remove Ads), and consumable diamond packs. All purchases are handled entirely by Apple through the App Store (StoreKit); we do not run our own store and we never receive your payment details, card number or billing address — Apple processes those under Apple's Privacy Policy. The app only receives confirmation of which product you bought and that the purchase succeeded, finishes each transaction on your device, and reports a record of the purchase to RevenueCat (below). See our Terms of Use for what each purchase gives you and how Restore Purchases works.

On Android, all purchases are handled entirely by Google through Google Play Billing; we never receive your payment details, card number or billing address — Google processes them under its own Privacy Policy. The app stores on your device only which product you bought and the purchase key (purchase token) Google Play provides, which prevents the same purchase from being delivered twice. To restore Remove Ads and Lifetime Unlimited, the app asks Google Play for the valid purchases of your account. When the app opens, the Google Play Billing library sends Google technical diagnostic logs about how the purchase connection is working (such as device model, Android version, language and country, mobile network code and app version); this is separate from the measurement in section 7 and from the consent you give for it.

Purchase records (RevenueCat): from version 1.5.0, so that we can see purchases and refunds quickly (Apple's and Google's reports arrive a day or more later), the app also reports every purchase to RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc., United States); when a purchase is reported, your earlier purchases in this app (for example Remove Ads) may also be reported to RevenueCat. The app contacts RevenueCat only when a purchase is reported; if you have not made a purchase, nothing is sent to RevenueCat. RevenueCat processes these records for us under a data processing agreement and confirms each purchase with Apple or Google Play. RevenueCat receives: which product was bought, its price and currency, the store's transaction number and the purchase date, the store (App Store or Google Play) and the store country, refunds, a random customer identifier that RevenueCat creates for this installation and, on iPhone and iPad, Apple's vendor identifier (IDFV, section 7); when the app connects, RevenueCat also sees your IP address. Your name, email address, payment details, leaderboard, Game Center or Play Games identity and advertising identifier (IDFA) are not sent to RevenueCat; on Android, no device identifier is sent to RevenueCat. RevenueCat does not decide what you own: Remove Ads and Lifetime Unlimited are still confirmed on your device with Apple or Google Play, and purchases work the same if RevenueCat cannot be reached. RevenueCat does not pass purchase information on to Meta, Google, AppsFlyer or PostHog (these ready-made connections are off). This record does not depend on the ad-measurement consent: RevenueCat receives every purchase. Where: on RevenueCat's servers in the United States (Amazon Web Services), that is, outside Türkiye (section 14). How long: as long as we use RevenueCat; we delete the records when we stop using it, and earlier if you ask us (section 13).

9. Sync and backup

When iCloud is turned on for the app on your device, the app uses Apple's iCloud key-value storage, part of your own iCloud account, to keep the following in sync across your devices and through a reinstall on the same iCloud account: your puzzle progress (the puzzles you have solved, the puzzles you have opened, your best scores and your daily puzzle results), your diamond balance and its transaction history, whether you have purchased Remove Ads, which colour themes you have unlocked, and your daily streak. This storage is operated by Apple under Apple's Privacy Policy; we cannot see this data. If iCloud is off, or you are signed out of iCloud, this data stays only on your device as described in "Data stored only on your device" above, and is lost if you delete the app. To stop this syncing, open your device's Settings app, tap your name, then iCloud, and turn iCloud off for the app (or turn off iCloud Drive generally).

On Android the app uses Android's own backup (Auto Backup). If backup with your Google account is on, Android copies two of the app's files to your Google account backup about once a day while the device is idle: the local database with your game data (progress, scores, diamond balance and transaction history, Remove Ads, unlocked themes, streak, settings) and a sync copy of them. Android restores these files when you reinstall the app with the same Google account or move to a new phone. Android, not we, makes the backup; we have no access to it. Changes after the last backup may not be restored. You can manage or delete the backup in Settings > Google > Backup. Lifetime Unlimited is not synced or restored through iCloud or Android backup: on each device it is confirmed with the App Store or Google Play (with "Restore Purchases" if needed).

10. Permissions

The app does not ask for access to your camera, microphone, photos, precise location or contacts. It asks for permission to send notifications only if you choose daily reminders (below). Only right after you choose "Katıl" (section 6), it asks for permission to use your device's advertising identifier under Apple's App Tracking Transparency, only to support the advertising and the measurement of our own ads described above; you can decline this at any time (see "App Tracking Transparency and non-personalised ads").

Daily reminders (optional). The app never asks for notification permission when you first open it. After you finish your first daily puzzle, it asks in its own words whether you would like a reminder; only if you agree does it show Apple's notification permission prompt. Reminders are local notifications: the app schedules them on your device, at most two a day (one at the time you choose and, if you have a streak of two days or more, one later that evening), only for days whose daily puzzle you have not solved yet. No server is involved, no push token is created, and nothing about your reminders or your play is sent to us or to anyone else. You can turn reminders off or change their time in Settings (Ayarlar) at any time, and you can withdraw the permission in iOS Settings or, on Android, in Settings > Apps > LINGRID > Notifications.

On Android, the app's release build declares 15 permissions. Seven are tied to features: internet access; vibration (haptic feedback); Google Play Billing; the advertising ID permission (AD_ID) used by the Google Mobile Ads SDK (the measurement SDKs in section 7 never collect the advertising ID); the Google Play Install Referrer permission (BIND_GET_INSTALL_REFERRER_SERVICE), which lets the app ask Google Play which link or ad led to the installation (section 7); on Android 13 and later, the notification permission (POST_NOTIFICATIONS), asked only if you choose daily reminders; and run-at-startup (RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED) so scheduled reminders survive a restart. The reminder permission is asked after the app's own explanation screen; no exact-alarm permission is requested, so a reminder may arrive a few minutes late. The other eight are technical permissions added by the advertising SDKs and Android libraries that show you no prompt: network state, wake lock, foreground service, Android's ad services (ADSERVICES: advertising ID, topics and attribution; three permissions), one permission added by Liftoff Monetize's SDK (com.amazon.privacypass.ATTEST) and one permission Android uses to keep the app's own internal messages private. There is no camera, microphone, location, contacts, photos or storage permission.

11. Network

Level puzzles, sounds and images are included in the app. The app also downloads small files of four kinds from our website on GitHub Pages (beydadonmezz.github.io/lingrid; versions before 1.4.0 use beydadonmezz.github.io/lingrid-site): the minimum supported app version, the recommended app version together with an optional short notice from us, new daily puzzles, and corrections to puzzle clues. These are plain downloads of public files: the app sends no account, device identifier or gameplay data with them. As with any web request, GitHub, Inc. processes technical request data such as your IP address to deliver the files. If the files cannot be downloaded, the app uses the copies included in it; without the recommended-version file, the app simply shows no update message. To tell you that a newer version is available, on iPhone and iPad the app asks Apple's public App Store lookup service (itunes.apple.com) at most once a day which version of LINGRID is current; the request contains only LINGRID's public app identifier and the store country. On Android the app asks Google Play, through Google Play's in-app update service on your device, whether an update is available; if you choose to update, Google Play downloads and installs it. These checks send no personal data beyond what every internet request carries: your IP address and the device's user agent (a technical description of the app and system). According to Google, its in-app update service uses device information, the app version and the list of the app's installed parts to find out whether an update is available, and does not pass this data on to third parties. The app connects to the internet for: Apple Game Center, as described above; Google's advertising and consent services, to request and show ads and to gather advertising consent, and, through AdMob mediation, Unity Ads and Liftoff Monetize, to bid for and show ads; Meta, Google Analytics for Firebase and AppsFlyer, for the measurement of our own ads described in section 7 (only after your consent, section 7); PostHog, for game statistics (only after "Katıl", sections 4 and 7); RevenueCat, for purchase records (section 8); and Apple's App Store/StoreKit and iCloud services, for purchases, the version check and sync. On Android the app also connects to Google Play for purchases, the installation source (section 7) and update checks, and to Google Play Games for leaderboards; backup is performed by Android. If you have joined the leaderboard, the app also connects to LINGRID's leaderboard server (Contabo, Germany, European Union; versions before 1.5.0 through Supabase, Frankfurt) for the data described in section 4. The app shows these legal texts without a connection. When you open their web version, your browser loads it from GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc., which processes technical request data such as your IP address to deliver the page. This website uses no cookies and no analytics.

If you report a clue with "Bu ipucu yanlış", the app opens your own email app with a prepared message (the puzzle number, the clue and the app version). Nothing is sent unless you send that email yourself; if you do, we receive your email address and what you wrote, and use them only to check and correct the clue and to reply to you.
